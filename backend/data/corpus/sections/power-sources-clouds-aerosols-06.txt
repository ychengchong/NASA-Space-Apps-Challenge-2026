POWER Cloud and Aerosol Data Sources: TEMPO
Publisher: NASA Langley Research Center, POWER Project

The Trace Gas and Cloud parameters described below originate from NASA's TEMPO mission — Tropospheric Emissions: Monitoring of Pollution and are available at the Hourly, Daily, and Monthly temporal levels. TEMPO is a direct spectroscopic measurement from a single instrument. It is an ultraviolet-visible grating spectrometer, launched in April 2023 as NASA's first Earth Venture Instrument and the agency's first science payload hosted aboard a commercial communications satellite, Intelsat 40e Zoogman et al., 2017. From a geostationary position near 91°W, TEMPO holds a fixed view of North America and sweeps the continent from east to west roughly once per hour throughout daylight. TEMPO also observes at fine spatial scale. The instrument's native ground sample is roughly 2 km north-south by 4.7 km east-west near the center of the field of regard, sufficient to resolve gradients across individual neighborhoods, industrial corridors, and transportation routes that coarser products average away. Level 3 products which POWER serves are gridded at a uniform 0.02° × 0.02°, somewhat finer than the native footprint. TEMPO measures backscattered sunlight across 290–490 nm and 540–740 nm. Trace gas abundances are inferred from the distinctive absorption signatures each species imprints on that spectrum, using differential optical absorption spectroscopy. Because the technique depends on sunlight traversing the atmosphere and returning to the instrument, TEMPO observes only during daylight and only when the atmospheric column is substantially free of cloud. With the exception of the ozone profile, every TEMPO parameter reports a vertical column density: the total quantity of a gas within a vertical path through the atmosphere.
