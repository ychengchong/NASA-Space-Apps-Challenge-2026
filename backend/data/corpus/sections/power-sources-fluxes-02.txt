Validation of the satellite-based values was conducted via comparisons to surface observations using the 1° gridded values. Surface reference networks such as Baseline Surface Radiation Network (BSRN) provide the high-accuracy ground truth used for these gridded comparisons. See Rutan et al., 2015 for in-depth validation of CERES. Further validation results are presented below for each processing source. The quality of surface-based measurements are generally considered more accurate than satellite-derived values. However, measurement uncertainties from instrument calibration drift, operational uncertainties, instrumental changes, or data gaps are often unknown or unreported for many surface data sets. Estimated instantaneous uncertainties for solar radiation measurements are on the order of 3-5% (Michalsky et al., 1999 Wild et al., 2013, Wild et al 2017) while "end-to-end" uncertainties can be much higher (11-21%) (Franssen et al., 2010). Specialized high-quality research sites such as those in the BSRN are estimated to be more accurate by a factor of two. BSRN was established specifically to provide research-grade surface radiation measurements with well-characterized uncertainties for the validation of satellite-derived and modeled fluxes.

It is noted that both the NASA SRB project and CERES SYN1deg focus on providing high-quality estimates of the Earth's top-of-atmosphere (TOA) and surface solar insolation in support of NASA's effort to quantify components of the Earth's radiation budget, while the focus of the CERES FLASHFlux project is to provide solar data within one week of satellite observations with well validated estimates of its accuracy. The POWER data sets concurrently concatenate these data sets. However, it is not recommended to use these fluxes to assess climate trends that encompass changes in the source data without a careful assessment and analysis that accounts for discontinuities and uncertainties in the parameter values. Because GEWEX SRB R4-IP and CERES SYN1deg are produced with different instruments, input cloud retrievals, and radiative transfer implementations, their concatenation at the January 1, 2001 boundary can introduce a step discontinuity that is not a physical climate signal.

Warning

The time series of daily surface energy fluxes data may include multiple data sources; accordingly, it is not recommended for use in assessing climate trends that encompass a source data change.
