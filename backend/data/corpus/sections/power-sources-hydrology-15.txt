POWER Hydrology Data Sources: Comparison to MERRA-2
Publisher: NASA Langley Research Center, POWER Project

Both products use the NASA Catchment land-surface model at their core. This table highlights the difference in the two products:

Aspect | MERRA-2 | SMAP L4

Land-surface model | Catchment (in GEOS DAS) | Catchment (standalone)

Grid | ½° × ⅝° | ~9 km EASE → 0.09° lat–lon

Satellite constraint | None (atmospheric only) | SMAP L-band brightness temps

Record start | 1981 | 2015

Temporal levels | Hourly–climatology | Daily–climatology

SMAP set a target unbiased RMSE of 0.04 m³/m³ for surface soil moisture, and L4_SM validation has reported skill near this requirement against in-situ core validation sites (Entekhabi et al., 2010).

- Strengths:

- ~6× finer spatial resolution than MERRA-2

- Extensive product assessments completed by SMAP team

- Percentile products can be used for drought classification.

- Limitations:

- Record begins in 2015 (MERRA-2 present for full POWER availability)

- Not quite global coverage: ~85°N/S latitude coverage

- Active radar lost July 2015

Because both products share the same underlying model physics, transitions between MERRA-2 and SMAP in a time series are physically consistent in character, though absolute magnitudes may differ due to the satellite constraint and grid resolution.
