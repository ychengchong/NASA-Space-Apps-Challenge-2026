# Record years

This note was written by the Trend Detective project. It explains the record years the app lists with a result. It is the project's own description, not a NASA method.

## What is listed

For every result with a rate, the app lists plain facts about the yearly values it used. These are the up to three years with the highest yearly values, highest first, and the up to three with the lowest, lowest first. Only years above the record's lowest value can be among the highest, and only years below its highest value among the lowest, so a record that is zero in most years, such as snow or sea ice, never calls its minimum a high. No year is in both lists. When years have equal values the earliest is listed first, and the app says how many more years share the last value listed. When every year has the same value, nothing is ranked.

## First and last ten years

With at least twenty analysis years, the app compares the plain mean of the first ten years used with the mean of the last ten, and gives the change between them. The two windows never share a year. A missing year is skipped, so a window can then span more than ten calendar years. With fewer than twenty years this comparison is left out.

## These are not tests

These facts describe the yearly values used. They are not tests, and their significance was not assessed. In a comparison of places the record years cover the common years.
