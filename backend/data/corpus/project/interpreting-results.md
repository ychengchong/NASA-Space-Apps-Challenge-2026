# Reading a result: what significance does and does not say

This note was written by the Trend Detective project. It explains how to read the app's results. It is the project's own guidance, not a NASA statement.

## Not significant is not proof of no change

A not significant verdict means the data do not provide sufficient evidence for a monotonic trend under the method. It does not establish that no change occurred. The test asks only about a monotonic trend, and its conservative design reduces its power, particularly for short records, so a real change can go undetected. A zero estimated rate does not prove no change either.

## Significant is not cause or importance

A significant verdict means the evidence for a monotonic trend meets the stated threshold under the method's assumptions. Significance is not practical importance: a statistically significant change can be small. Significance is not cause: the test says nothing about why the series changed, and it does not attribute a change to any process or activity. A significant result also does not certify that the method's assumptions hold for the real series.

## P-values and intervals

A p-value is the probability of a statistic at least as extreme as the one observed under the null model and its assumptions. It is not the probability that a claim is true. A 95% interval describes how often the procedure would cover the true value over repeated use; it does not assign a probability to a fixed true value. P-values alone do not describe every form of uncertainty.

## Comparing places

A comparison of places is descriptive. Each place is fitted on the same complete years, but no test of the difference between the places' rates was performed. One significant and one not significant result do not establish that the rates differ. For two places the app also describes the gap, the first place's yearly value minus the second's, with its own descriptive rate; that difference was not tested either.

## Scans and maps

When many periods are tried, selection and multiple testing affect what a result means, so a favourable period found by scanning is never confirmation. The app's period explorer is descriptive and does not report significance. Regional maps show descriptive rates for each grid cell and do not assess significance.
